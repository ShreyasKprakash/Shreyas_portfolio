TACOMA

Metallic tank first, composite dome after

Wasserstoff-Tanks in Composite-Bauweise · LuFo VI, Airbus-led with DLR, Fraunhofer, INVENT, Uni Stuttgart

dlr.de — TACOMA project →

  • The current work is the metallic baseline tank. It gets built and tested first, and the composite version follows from what we learn on it.
  • I support the manufacturer on the drawings and go through the build before anything is made. If something on their side does not match what we need on the Lufthansa side, I raise it then rather than after the part exists.
  • I draw the base P&IDs for the tank and the systems around it.
  • I write the specifications for the components on the cryogenic hydrogen side: valves, control valves, pressure regulators, metal seals, burst discs and the safety devices. I go through manufacturer data, check it against what we actually need, and order. Most recently the gas panels for the hydrogen supply.
  • The instrumentation chain is mine too: pressure in absolute, relative and differential form, plus temperature, fill level and vacuum-integrity monitoring, and the data that comes back from it.
  • The structural health monitoring runs on the metallic tank first. Piezoelectric guided-wave and acoustic-emission sensors, with Fibre Bragg Grating and electrical resistance as the other methods we looked at.
  • In the composite step the metal dome is replaced by a composite one, built by INVENT with DLR. That brings in a second manufacturer and a new set of interfaces at the dome joint.
  • I keep the interface control documents current across the partners, and flag manufacturing and integration issues as I find them.

M.Sc. thesis

Flow diagnostics on a hydrogen-evolving cell

Institute of Reactive Flows & Diagnostics, TU Darmstadt · Prof. Dr. habil. Andreas Dreizler

I measured the flow that hydrogen bubbles drive in an alkaline electrolysis cell. I commissioned the optical rig myself, worked out the seeding, and captured both phases at once — the bubbles by shadowgraphy, the electrolyte by tracer particles in a laser sheet — then separated them in post-processing and tracked them with PTV. The result: the electrode surface geometry changed the mean jet velocity by a factor of four in an otherwise identical cell. The thesis also documents where the method runs out, mainly that the flow is three-dimensional and particles leave the light sheet.

Composite structures for two flight vehicles

Structures & integration · Mar 2024 – Mar 2026

  • I designed the CFRP and GFRP layups for the body structure. Glass rather than carbon around the avionics and payload, because carbon is conductive and the antennas have to see out.
  • The body tube was filament wound. I took it through manufacture, cure and post-processing with the university's composite workshop.
  • FEA in Ansys Workbench: static, transient, modal and buckling. The margins of safety I documented were what the team needed before parts could go for manufacture.
  • I set the load cases the structure had to survive: motor thrust in compression, parachute shock in tension, and the vehicle bending on its rail supports while it sits horizontally.
  • The coupler between motor mount, airframe and avionics bay was mine from start to finish. Several iterations driven by the analysis, printed in AlSi10Mg, measured against the drawing, mechanically tested, then flown.
  • I also controlled the mechanical interfaces with propulsion, avionics and recovery, and wrote the structures sections of the reports that went to the competition's evaluation board.
